# Scope Evolution Chain condition text to common cases, with a generic fallback

PokeAPI's `evolution-chain` resource records far more evolution triggers and conditions than a Pokédex UI can reasonably spell out: level, friendship, beauty, held items, known moves, time of day, and trade are common; battle-style moves, recoil damage, three critical hits in one battle, a defeated Bisharp nearby, region-specific mechanics, and several others exist for a handful of very specific species. `DescribeEvolutionCondition` (see `internal/pokemon/evolution.go`) only recognizes the common set and falls back to the generic "special condition" for everything else, rather than trying to describe every PokeAPI condition field.

This mirrors the Pokédex Entry's own precedent (see `docs/adr/0003-prefer-generation-1-pokedex-entry-version.md`): favor a well-understood common case over exhaustively modeling PokeAPI's full data shape, and degrade honestly rather than guessing. A generic fallback is a small, deliberate loss of detail on a handful of species (Shedinja's "spare Poké Ball & party space" and the level/friendship/beauty/item/trade/known-move/time-of-day cases above are the only conditions explicitly described) rather than either an ever-growing pile of special cases for increasingly rare mechanics, or a wrong guess presented as if it were a real condition.

The same reasoning extends to *which* `evolution_details` entry a branch uses when PokeAPI records more than one (e.g. Pikachu evolves into Raichu via a Thunder Stone in every version group, but only into Alolan Raichu in Sun/Moon): `BuildEvolutionChain` prefers Red, Blue, then Yellow, in that order, falling back to the first entry PokeAPI listed — the exact same `evolutionDetailVersionGroupPriority` list and fallback behavior as `pokedexEntryVersionPriority`, kept consistent with the rest of the app's Gen-1 styling rather than introducing a second, different selection rule.

## Considered options

- **Model every PokeAPI evolution trigger and condition field**: most complete, but a large, ever-growing surface for conditions that apply to at most one or two species each (three critical hits, a nearby defeated Bisharp, a Gimmighoul coin count, ...); rejected as disproportionate effort for the value it adds.
- **Show the raw PokeAPI trigger name verbatim when a condition isn't recognized** (e.g. "three-critical-hits") rather than "special condition": more information per rare case, but exposes PokeAPI's internal slug naming directly to the player, inconsistent with how every other piece of PokeAPI data in this app is normalized into human-readable text first; rejected for breaking that consistency over a handful of edge cases.
